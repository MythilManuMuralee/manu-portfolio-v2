Frameworks

Nine frameworks behind the work, plus published research. Each one is the reasoning a problem was put through, rather than a description of what got done.

01

Friction-First Commercial Strategy

Applies to: FourEM, the $3.5M Kraft paper trade line

This framework helps find highly profitable business opportunities by simply looking for what is broken in a market. Instead of fighting competitors, it looks for places where goods, money, or information are getting stuck. By solving that specific bottleneck, like connecting raw material suppliers directly to buyers who need them, we can unlock commercial value and immediate revenue.

Traditional strategy frameworks assume a clean execution environment. I start from the opposite premise: every market has embedded friction, and identifying that friction is the first step to unlocking massive value. I used this exact logic to identify a supply gap in the SAARC paper market, bypassing traditional retail competition to build an upstream trade line that turned over $3.5M in 9 months.

The process

  1. System Mapping. Document the existing value chain without assumptions. Understand exactly how goods, capital, or data flow from origin to destination.
  2. Gap Identification. Locate the specific friction point. This could be a geographic supply shortage, a logistics bottleneck, or a severe information asymmetry.
  3. Operational Architecture. Do not just advise; build the bridge. Leverage cross-border networks and structure the financial logistics required to connect supply directly with demand.
  4. Value Capture. Launch the solution, manage the counterparty risks, and measure the commercial impact directly in revenue and profit margins.

02

Data-to-Signal Pipeline

Applies to: Krea, GSRV and the Hyundai España work

This translates large, messy datasets, whether ten years of road infrastructure records or 500 primary market surveys, into the specific number an executive actually needs: a cost of acquisition, a payback period, a return on investment.

Data without a narrative is just noise. Whether the raw material is a decade of NHAI road data processed in ArcGIS or 500+ consumer interviews behind a funding raise, the goal is the same: translate a large, messy dataset into a business recommendation an executive can act on.

The process

  1. Raw Aggregation. Gather large, unstructured datasets from primary and secondary sources, keeping data lineage and integrity intact.
  2. Spatial and Temporal Coding. Structure and geo-process the data to reveal geographic, historical or demographic patterns that were not visible in the raw form.
  3. Econometric Modelling. Apply statistical tests and feasibility models to strip out noise and bias and isolate the signal that is actually mathematically sound.
  4. Executive Synthesis. Translate the econometric findings into the specific KPIs, CAC and payback period among them, that a decision-maker can act on directly.

03

Scaled Climate
Unit Economics

Applies to: Vaayubon

Climate projects often fail because they rely on charity. This model approaches climate repair through fintech and platform economics instead: eliminate the cost of the raw material, secure forward guarantees to buy the resulting carbon credits, and let fixed monitoring costs fall as the network grows, so every additional farmer improves the margin rather than the budget.

Hard tech requires hard economics. Co-founding Vaayubon, I designed a model built on both physical output, biochar, and carbon credits. An AI-enabled layer of IoT sensors and satellite data for measurement, reporting and verification sits on the roadmap rather than built, and would make issuance auditable at scale.

The process

  1. Farmer Revenue Moat. Purchase agricultural waste that would otherwise be burned, giving smallholder farmers an alternate source of income and securing the feedstock supply chain.
  2. Forward Offtake First. Rather than produce credits and hope for buyers, the model is built on securing forward offtake agreements with global buyers before production, so demand is locked in ahead of the cash burn.
  3. Digital MRV Architecture. Carbon credits are financial instruments whose value depends entirely on trust. The architecture for an end-to-end digital measurement, reporting and verification layer is designed and sits on the roadmap.
  4. Aggregation Before Production. The feedstock base is secured first: 150+ farmers aggregated in Anantapur under supply agreements. The conversion facility is in build-out and no biochar has been produced yet, so the unit economics remain modelled rather than realised.

04

Factor-Neutral
Mean Reversion

Applies to: Factor-Neutral Pairs Trading Pipeline

This engine runs statistical arbitrage by neutralising broader market risk. It first uses unsupervised machine learning to prove two companies are fundamentally identical twins, then runs econometric tests to prove that any price gap between them is a temporary anomaly that should snap back to its historical average, regardless of whether the wider market is rising or falling.

Naive correlation in markets is dangerous: two seemingly correlated assets can diverge permanently once the structure underneath them changes. The edge comes from combining fundamental constraints with strict econometric cointegration testing. I built a production-grade pairs trading pipeline on this logic, screening 503 S&P 500 tickers into 118,833 fundamental twin pairs and isolating the ones that genuinely mean-revert.

The process

  1. Unsupervised Clustering. DBSCAN groups stocks by fundamentals rather than forcing every point into a cluster the way K-Means would, so outliers are not erroneously matched into a pair.
  2. Engle-Granger, Stage One. An OLS regression models one stock's price against its candidate twin; the resulting beta coefficient defines the hedge ratio.
  3. Engle-Granger, Stage Two. The regression residuals, the pricing error, are tested for stationarity with an Augmented Dickey-Fuller test, which is what proves cointegration rather than assumes it.
  4. Signal and Execution. Dynamic Z-score bands on the residual spread generate entry and exit signals only when the deviation from historical equilibrium is statistically meaningful.

05

High-Frequency
Volatility Modelling

Applies to: High-Frequency Volatility Forecasting

This tests whether machine learning can anticipate volatility regime shifts faster than traditional parametric models. Forecasting 5-minute realised volatility on E-mini S&P 500 futures, the pipeline is built specifically to prevent the look-ahead bias that undermines a lot of financial ML work. The finding is a clean null result: under strict walk-forward validation and block bootstrap testing, the ML model does not beat the parametric baseline by a statistically significant margin.

Predicting market direction is hard, but volatility is persistent and forecastable. The problem is that during sudden shocks, classic models like GARCH adapt too slowly. This pipeline tests whether a non-linear XGBoost model, or a GARCH-XGBoost hybrid, closes that latency gap.

The process

  1. Data and Target. E-mini S&P 500 futures sampled at 5-minute intervals; the target is rolling realised volatility over a 288-bar window.
  2. Feature Design. Lagged RV, rolling means and standard deviations, VIX levels, and cyclical time-of-day encodings.
  3. Asymmetric Loss. Under-forecasting volatility is the economically costly error, so the XGBoost loss function penalises it three times harder than over-forecasting.
  4. GARCH Baseline. Fit exclusively on the training fold, with strictly causal one-step-ahead forecasts, so the baseline cannot see the future either.
  5. Hybrid Ensemble. A 50/50 blend, since GARCH captures long-term unconditional variance and XGBoost captures non-linear short-term shocks.
  6. Strategy Overlay. A volatility-targeting overlay converts each forecast into a position size, so the test measures economic value, not just forecast accuracy.
  7. Validation Design. Expanding-window walk-forward across three folds, plus an explicit regime-shift diagnostic.
  8. The Honest Result. A 1,000-iteration block bootstrap found the Sharpe spread between the XGBoost overlay and the GARCH baseline was not statistically significant, the 95% confidence interval spanned zero. Reported as a clean null result rather than reframed as a win.

06

Macro Regime
Detection

Applies to: Macroeconomic Regime Analysis

Rather than relying on subjective human labels like recession or expansion, this model lets the data define the state of the economy. Compressing dozens of macro indicators into a three-dimensional space, it clusters historical periods into hidden regimes, then shifts portfolio weights depending on which regime the economy is currently in.

Markets price assets differently depending on the macro environment, but regimes are not directly observable, and static allocations ignore them entirely. This is a purely data-driven, unsupervised approach to discovering those underlying states and allocating tactically in response.

The process

  1. Feature Engineering. Raw standardised macro indicators from FRED carry secular trends that distort ML algorithms, so rolling averages and growth rates strip the trend out first.
  2. Autoencoder Architecture. Sliding windows of the detrended features feed a dense autoencoder with a symmetric 16→8→3→8→16 bottleneck.
  3. Reconstruction Error as Signal. The autoencoder is trained to reconstruct its own input; a high reconstruction error flags an anomalous or stressed period.
  4. Gaussian Mixture Clustering. A GMM clusters the clean, three-dimensional latent space rather than the noisy raw indicators, which is what makes the regimes separable.
  5. Regime-Conditional Allocation. Historical asset returns are analysed under each regime, and portfolio weights shift dynamically as the regime shifts.
  6. Why Unsupervised. Hand-labelled regimes, NBER dates for example, are retrospective and rigid. This discovers the regimes from the data instead of assuming them.

07

Agentic AI Orchestration

Applies to: Wall-Et

This system uses artificial intelligence to completely remove manual data entry from software tools. Instead of forcing users to click through menus or type out forms, it allows them to just speak naturally or snap a photo of a document. The AI orchestration automatically reads, understands, and categorizes that unstructured data into usable financial insights, saving thousands of hours and preventing human error.

Most enterprise AI implementations fail because they bolt chatbots onto broken processes. True digital transformation requires removing the friction of data entry entirely. When building Wall‑Et, my goal was not to build a smarter calculator, but to architect a workflow that transforms unstructured inputs (voice and photos) into structured financial insights, reducing the user's cognitive load to zero.

The process

  1. Friction Audit. Map the current workflow. Identify exactly where human effort is highest in cost but lowest in strategic value.
  2. Logic Architecture. Design the decision trees and the human-in-the-loop handoffs before writing a single line of code.
  3. API Orchestration. Connect LLMs, voice-processing models, and databases using low-code tools to create a seamless, end-to-end reasoning pipeline.
  4. Iterative Deployment. Launch rapidly through prototyping. Measure the adoption rate, test assumptions against real user data, and refine the workflow instantly.

08

System Shift Investment Logic

Applies to: System Shift Portfolio

This model fixes the biggest problem with "green" investing: ignoring the supply chain. Before evaluating a company, it scans the entire ecosystem of suppliers and logistics that support it to ensure true alignment with global environmental limits. By mathematically diversifying capital across the entire supply chain, this framework provides investors with both robust risk protection and meaningful, measurable sustainability.

ESG investing is fundamentally flawed when it only looks at end-products. A green company cannot survive if its supply chain collapses. I developed the System Shift Portfolio (SSP) framework based on the Earth System Boundaries model. It proves that sustainability and profitable risk-hedging are not mutually exclusive when you invest in the entire ecosystem.

The process

  1. Boundary Definition. Set rigid ecological constraints using the Earth System Boundaries framework alongside traditional risk tolerance parameters.
  2. Stakeholder Mapping. Evaluate the entire supply chain. Look beyond the final consumer product to include the raw material suppliers and intermediate logistics.
  3. Risk Hedging. Diversify investments across this entire ecosystem to ensure that a failure in one node does not collapse the entire portfolio.
  4. Impact Compounding. Run constrained optimizations to find the portfolio allocation that maximizes risk-adjusted returns while driving compounding environmental resilience.

09

Externalities Internalisation Logic

Applies to: Value Balancing Alliance capstone

Carbon has a market price because regulators forced one into existence. Water, waste, air pollution and land use mostly do not. This framework carries the same convergence logic that priced carbon, market price moving toward the true social cost, across to the drivers that have not been priced yet, and produces a number for how far each one still has to move.

The Value Balancing Alliance and Deloitte built a benchmark for carbon: the Social Cost of Carbon against the EU ETS market price gives a measurable internalisation rate today, and a projected one for 2050. The capstone tests whether that same logic, market price converging on social cost, generalises to water, waste, air and water pollution and land use, anchored on IPCC AR6 WG2 Chapter 4 for the water estimate specifically.

The process

  1. Goal. Give corporates, regulators and investors a way to anticipate the trajectory of environmental costs as externalities become internalities.
  2. Outcome. A comparative framework of 2025 versus 2050 internalisation rates across the full set of environmental impact drivers the VBA Environmental P&L tracks.
  3. Outputs. A regulatory review, a Social Cost estimate for water consumption anchored on IPCC AR6, quantified current and projected 2050 internalisation rates, and a payback-period model for proactive corporate investment.
  4. Activities. Literature and regulatory review, structured interviews with VBA methodology experts, mapping of regulatory instruments, and scenario modelling across regional water-stress contexts.
